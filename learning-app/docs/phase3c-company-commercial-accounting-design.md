# Phase 3C company purchase accounting boundary

Status: isolated, un-deployed company capture/allocation, reversal-notice inbox, paid-seat access-provenance, held-liability reversal, conservative paid-seat source-revocation, free-enrollment-claim, free-company-assignment-origin, read-only access-assessment, and atomic-reversal migrations are drafted. They do not create spendable instructor earnings, revoke shared enrollment access, or assert live-company accounting readiness. Migrations 43–52 pass together in a disposable PostgreSQL 17.5 PGlite fixture, with the free-enrollment and free-company-assignment functions exercised as the source of access evidence. Migrations 43–44 also passed isolated PostgreSQL 18.3 checks. Production reports PostgreSQL 17.6. These are **not** full Supabase-schema deployment tests.

## Current behavior and the mismatch

- One company purchase pays for one course and **many employee seats** in one Paystack transaction. The purchase records its unit price, total, selected employees, and the successful payment attempt. Finalization grants each employee access.
- Learner commerce instead uses one `learning_orders` row per learner/course/charge. Its capture ledger, commercial allocation, earnings release, refund case, dispute case, and payout paths all key off that order.
- A company purchase currently creates no marketplace capture ledger, commission allocation, or instructor/provider earning. Creating one learner order for the *whole* company charge would misstate per-seat sales; creating ordinary learner orders without company provenance would expose a company-funded sale to personal learner views and make refund/payout handling ambiguous.

## Required model before a live company charge

1. Snapshot the selling entity, instructor/payee, commercial-terms version, course, NGN unit amount, seat list and total when the company manager prepares a purchase. For an organization-owned course, the organization owner is the provisional payee snapshot; team members' personal-course earnings must not appear in organization reports. Confirm the provider payout policy before release.
2. Record **one** Paystack capture for the full company purchase, keyed by provider domain, reference and transaction ID. Allocate its exact total to immutable per-seat sale lines (`unit amount × seat count = capture amount`). Never treat Paystack's charge amount plus fees as the course price.
3. Post balanced platform-share and seller-liability entries from the seat lines. The company owner/admin sees its own invoice and seats; the selling instructor or provider sees only its own attributed sales. Company staff must not see Growvelt's commission or an instructor's unrelated earnings.
4. Make webhook and callback reconciliation idempotent. Replaying the same verified charge must create neither extra seats nor extra accounting lines. A mismatched amount, currency, domain, reference or transaction ID must stop fulfillment and raise an operator review.
5. Keep company-origin earnings on hold until the charge's refund/dispute window and company reversal policy are implemented. Existing learner payout functions must not reserve or transfer a company earning merely because a learner-style hold date elapsed.
6. Model Paystack's **partial refund on one transaction** as a company purchase adjustment tied to affected seat lines, with proportional seller/platform reversals and the correct employee access consequence. A full refund or lost dispute must reverse the entire sale exactly once. A pending refund is not a completed reversal. See [Paystack refund documentation](https://paystack.com/docs/payments/refunds/) and [dispute documentation](https://paystack.com/docs/payments/manage-disputes/).
7. Only after capture, allocation, reversal, privacy, payout-hold, reconciliation and recovery tests pass may a service-only database capability return `true` from `is_learning_company_live_accounting_ready`. The checkout route fails closed when this function is absent, errors, or returns anything else. The environment switch remains a separate operator-controlled gate.

The draft `20260944000000` migration covers items 1–3 at the **held-liability** stage and an idempotent posting/reconciliation path. It does not expose company proceeds through the existing personal-earnings or payout functions. It intentionally leaves historical Test purchases with no terms snapshot unallocated and reportable for manual review. Items 5–7, including company refunds/disputes and controlled earnings release, remain open.

Draft migration `20260945000000` adds a private, append-only inbox for signed company refund/dispute notices. The webhook records a digest and minimal provider identifiers before acknowledging and sends an idempotent operator alert. This is **not** evidence that Paystack completed a reversal: no sale, balance, assignment, enrollment or payout changes occur. The inbox must be deployed before the webhook code; otherwise company reversal notices fail closed for retry. A later verified outcome processor must map a partial refund to exact paid seat lines and preserve an employee's other valid access (personal purchase or another company entitlement) before revoking anything.

Draft migration `20260946000000` records the assignment and enrollment tied to each newly granted paid company seat, plus whether each was already active before that purchase. Repeated grants are idempotent; a seat marked refunded or charged back cannot be granted again by replay. The record is private and immutable except for a future verified reversal transition. It neither decides whether an employee has another current entitlement nor revokes any access. Historical paid Test purchases have no reliable provenance and are surfaced by the reconciliation function for reviewed backfill or exclusion, not guessed from shared enrollment rows.

The pure `app/lib/payments/company-reversal-plan.ts` preflight accepts an explicitly selected set of paid seat lines only when their captured totals, domain, reference, NGN currency, sale split, and active provenance match the reported outcome. It rejects non-seat-sized partial amounts, missing or duplicate seats, and a lost dispute that does not cover the entire charge. This is deliberately **not** a refund/dispute processor: it does not verify Paystack's final state, deduplicate provider cases across attempts, post reversal entries, release or reserve money, or change access. Those checks must be added at the trusted service/database boundary before any live action. Run `npm run test:company-learning:reversal-plan` for its local edge-case checks.

The read-only company reversal lookup in `app/lib/payments/paystack.ts` fetches a refund or dispute by provider case ID under the configured Paystack domain. Its parsers require the expected charge transaction ID, company reference where Paystack supplies it, domain, NGN currency, and a valid amount. Only a fetched `processed` refund or `resolved`/`merchant-accepted` dispute yields a final reversal outcome; pending events never do. Paystack's refund fetch example supplies a numeric transaction ID but no reference, so the known ID is mandatory. This lookup is not yet connected to any mutation or automatic webhook processing. See [Paystack Fetch Refund](https://paystack.com/docs/api/refund/) and [Fetch Dispute](https://paystack.com/docs/api/dispute/). Run `npm run test:company-learning:provider-reversal-verification` for local parser checks.

Draft migration `20260947000000` records an exact-seat, append-only commercial reversal against held company proceeds. It requires a final reversal notice plus the trusted service's fetched provider status, charge ID, amount, and selected seat IDs; the database independently compares these with immutable sale lines and active paid-seat provenance. A processed partial refund can reverse whole seats whose sum matches the provider amount; a lost dispute must cover the entire charge. Provider-case replay returns the original posting, while a different case cannot reverse the same seat again. Reversal entries offset platform commission, held seller liability, and Paystack receivable; reconciliation checks each account and seat sum. This is still a **draft service-only primitive**, not an automatic processor. It cannot verify Paystack by itself, settle Paystack funds, handle seller proceeds that have been released/paid, or change an enrollment. Do not invoke it for a production charge until the trusted verification workflow, settlement policy, payout-release guard, access consequences, and full-schema tests are complete.

Draft migration `20260948000000` idempotently marks only the exact company-paid seat sources as refunded or charged back after a posted reversal. It records whether a shared assignment or enrollment remains and surfaces those rows as `company_reversal_shared_access_review_required`. It **does not cancel** those shared rows: an employee can acquire an independent/free grant after the purchase, and the existing enrollment may be reused without recording a new source. The purchase-time snapshot therefore cannot prove current exclusivity. This conservative step prevents accidental loss of valid learning/progress but may leave access active after a reversal. Before live company checkout, add source-aware access authorization or a reviewed access-resolution workflow, then test the free→paid transition, personal purchase, overlapping company seats, progress preservation, and refunds/disputes. No automatic reversal processor or live capability is enabled by this migration.

Draft migration `20260949000000` records each future successful self-enrollment while a course is truly free as an immutable independent claim. The grant is recorded even when a company purchase already created an active or completed enrollment row; repeated free-enrollment calls do not duplicate it or reset progress. Paid and limited-time-free courses cannot create such a claim. Existing enrollment rows are **not** backfilled: their original grant source may be unknowable. This is one input for a later source-aware access resolver, not itself permission to cancel a shared enrollment. Historical free assignments and personal grants still need source review.

Draft migration `20260950000000` records the free origin of each future successful company-manager assignment, including a free re-selection when the assignment and enrollment rows were originally created by a paid company seat. The origin is immutable and private; repeat selections do not duplicate it. It applies only while the course is published and free and the manager/employee are active members. An active assignment without an active/completed enrollment does not gain a free-origin record. Historical assignments are not guessed. A future resolver must check that the assignment and company membership remain active before treating this as an access source; the existence of an old origin alone is insufficient.

Draft migration `20260951000000` adds a service-only, read-only assessment of each already-applied reversal. It returns `retain_independent_source` for a recorded free self-claim, a still-active free company assignment and membership, an active personal entitlement, or another active paid company seat. Unknown pre-existing access, a different active assignment without proven origin, a paid personal order without an active entitlement, a completed enrollment, or a currently free course returns `manual_review`. An inactive enrollment returns `already_inactive`. Only an active enrollment with none of these signals returns `unproven_exclusive_candidate`. The candidate label is **not permission to cancel**: source grants can race with reversal processing, and historical/legacy origins require full-schema review. No route calls this function automatically, and it changes no access.

Draft migration `20260952000000` wraps the commercial reversal and paid-seat source marking in one service-only database call. A failure in the second step rolls back the ledger posting; replays return the existing result. The service loses direct execute permission on the two separate operations, so it cannot intentionally leave a posted reversal without its seat-source result. The trusted service must still fetch and verify the final Paystack case independently before invoking this wrapper; the database cannot authenticate a caller-provided provider outcome. This does not cancel shared access, release company seller funds, or enable live checkout.

The operator-only `POST /api/admin/payments/company-reversal/commit-test` route is **disabled unless** `PAYMENTS_TEST_COMPANY_REVERSAL_COMMIT_ENABLED=true` is deliberately set. It accepts an inbox event ID and exact selected employee IDs, requires same-origin and a Growvelt Learning admin session, and handles Test Mode only. It re-fetches the final case from Paystack under the configured Test key, checks the notice, sale, amount, selected paid seat lines and current provenance, then calls migration 52's atomic wrapper. A matching replay is read-only; a changed selection conflicts. It never initiates a refund/dispute, never changes shared enrollment access, and is not a live-domain recovery path. Keep the flag unset until migrations 43–52 pass full-schema testing and a reviewed operator workflow is ready. The remaining shared-access review and company seller payout policy still block live-company accounting readiness.

The reusable isolated check is `scripts/test-phase3c-company-ledger-local.cjs`. It requires `@electric-sql/pglite` as a local test-only package (or `PGLITE_PACKAGE_PATH` pointing to an extracted copy; v0.3.15 was used for PostgreSQL 17.5). It reads migrations 43–52 from this project's deployable `supabase/migrations` directory. It verifies two seat lines, exact rounding, balanced capture/allocation and reversal ledgers, idempotence, append-only records, historical-purchase detection, amount/domain rejection, reversal-inbox replay/conflict/privacy behavior, paid-seat provenance replay guards, atomic posting rollback, and dynamic access decisions. Free self-enrollment and free company assignment claims in this combined fixture are produced through their real grant functions, including reuse of an existing paid-created enrollment/assignment. It never connects to Supabase. A full local Supabase stack was unavailable on the test host because Docker is not installed; this isolated fixture cannot establish full-schema compatibility or production readiness.

## Test cases required for the accounting migration

- One verified company transaction with three seats produces one capture, three immutable seat sale lines and an exactly balanced split. Replaying webhook and callback leaves all counts and balances unchanged.
- Mismatched domain, reference, transaction ID, NGN amount, seat count or course terms cannot post a sale or grant additional access.
- Personal instructor, organization owner, organization team member, company manager, employee and admin queries each reveal only their permitted financial data.
- Refund one seat, refund all seats, lost dispute, duplicate refund/dispute events and refund after an earning becomes available/paid each preserve a balanced ledger and correct access. Release and payout are blocked while an unresolved company case exists.
- Existing learner orders and the confirmed company **Test Mode** purchase remain readable and unchanged. Historical company Test purchases require an explicit reviewed accounting backfill or an auditable exclusion; do not silently apply today's commercial terms to yesterday's sale.
